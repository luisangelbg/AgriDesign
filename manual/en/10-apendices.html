AFile and table formats

1Introduction to AgriDesign

✓Decision rule · reading a P valueSection 1.9
P valueEvidence against equal meansHow to write it
P < 0.001Very strong“The treatment effect was highly significant.”
0.001 ≤ P < 0.01Strong“The effect was significant.”
0.01 ≤ P < 0.05Moderate“There was evidence of differences”; it is worth confirming in another season or location.
P ≥ 0.05Insufficient“No differences were detected.” Do not claim that the treatments are equal: there may not have been enough replicates.

Always report the exact P value, the F with its degrees of freedom and the effect size (the means with their standard errors, or η²), not just whether the result is significant.

B1Home, simulator and theory

✓Decision rule · block or not?Section 2.2
If the experimental units…then…
are in the field, on land with uneven slope, moisture or historyRandomised complete blocks, with the blocks perpendicular to the gradient.
have two crossed gradients (rows and columns, animals and periods)Latin square, if the number of treatments allows it.
are handled in different batches, days or lotsEach batch is a block, even if the land is uniform.
are pots on a uniform bench or animals of the same weight and ageComplete randomisation; you gain degrees of freedom and lose nothing.
are so many that a block cannot hold all the treatmentsIncomplete blocks (lattices, alpha designs), Block 5.

When in doubt, block: the cost is r − 1 degrees of freedom; the benefit can be half the error.

✓Decision rule · from the field layout to the designSection 2.4
Your experiment has…DesignTypical case
One factor, homogeneous units, no gradientCompletely randomisedPots on a bench; animals of the same weight
One factor, one known gradientRandomised complete blocksSlope of the land; litter or barn; sampling day
One factor, two gradientsLatin squareRows × columns of the field; animals × periods
Two or more factors studied togetherFactorial, completely randomised or in blocksVariety × fertiliser; breed × diet
One factor on main plots and another within themSplit-plotIrrigation × varieties; pasture × stocking rate
Many treatments, blocks that cannot hold them allIncomplete blocks (lattice, alpha)Variety trials with 20 to 100 entries
A numeric variable measured before treatmentAny of the above + covariateInitial weight; plant stand; soil analysis

This is the same table the app shows under Choosing the design from the layout; the Block 5 chapter develops it design by design.

B2Data and variable roles

✓Decision rule · how the app proposes the role of a columnSection 3.4
If the column…the app proposes…
is called Block, Rep, Replicate, Bloque, Repetición or R (in any combination of upper and lower case)Block / replicate
is called Row, Fila, Hilera, Renglón · Col, Column, ColumnaRow · Column
is called Plot, Parcela, ID, Sample, Muestra, Obs… and all its values are distinctIdentifier / plot
is text with up to 40 levelsTreatment factor
is text with all its values distinct, or with more than 40 categoriesIdentifier / plot · Exclude
is numeric with a few integer values, each repeated almost the same number of times, and is not named like a measurementTreatment factor, labelled Integer codes (n levels)
is numeric with few values, but with unequal counts or a measurement name (score, yield, height, peso, altura…)Response (Y), with the flag integer codes → factor?
is called Cov, Covariate, Covariable, Initial, Inicial, Stand…Covariate (ANCOVA)
is numeric and has no variationExclude, labelled Constant
is numeric and none of the previous rules appliesResponse (Y)

These are proposals. A column with codes 1, 2, 3, 4 for the varieties looks numeric: if the app leaves it as a response, change it to a factor. And the other way round: a score from 1 to 9 with even counts may be taken for treatment codes.

B3Descriptive statistics

✓Decision rule · reading the ANOVA CV in agricultural trialsSection 4.3
CVReadingTypical of…
< 10%Excellent precisionPlant height, phenology, greenhouse and laboratory trials.
10 to 20%Normal in the fieldGrain yield in well-managed field plots.
20 to 30%High; precision is limitedYield under difficult conditions, counts, quality variables.
> 30%Very high; checkInsect counts, disease severity, or a trial with problems: heterogeneous land, lost plots, typing errors, a variable that calls for a transformation.

The app applies these cut-offs to the cards and to the interpretation: green up to 20%, amber up to 30%, red above. They are benchmarks for yield; every variable has its own.

✓Decision rule · reading the profiles by blockSection 4.4
If the lines…then…
are nearly parallel and vertically separatedBlocking captured a real difference between blocks and the additive model is reasonable. This is what a good trial looks like.
are nearly parallel and overlapThe blocks do not differ; blocking cost no more than a few degrees of freedom.
cross at a single pointA suspicious plot. Look it up in the field book before going on.
cross all over the placeBlock × treatment interaction: the ranking of the treatments changes from block to block. Check additivity in Block 4; a transformation may be needed.

The same reading applies to the interaction plot between two factors, except that there the interaction may be the very result you are looking for.

B4Assumptions, transformations and non-parametric tests

✓Decision rule · the grade of the diagnosisSection 5.2
GradeWhenWhat to do
AAll checks green.Go on to Block 5 with the original scale.
BOne amber warning.The ANOVA is acceptable. Mention the check in the methods; if the warning concerns variances, be careful with the mean comparisons.
COne red check, or two amber warnings.Compare the transformations or use the non-parametric route, and report which you chose and why.
DTwo or more red checks.A transformation will hardly fix everything: rank-based procedures are the safe option, or a generalised linear model for counts and proportions.

Each test is coloured according to its P value: green if P ≥ α, amber if α/5 ≤ P < α (between 0.01 and 0.05 with α = 0.05) and red if P < α/5.

✓Decision rule · what to do with a suspicious plotSection 5.2
If in the field book you find…then…
a typing error (a misplaced decimal point, two transposed digits)Correct the value in the file and reload it.
a plot damaged by animals, flooding or irrigation failure, documentedDeclare it missing (empty cell) and say so in the methods.
nothing abnormalThe value stays. If it is very influential, analyse with and without it and report both.

A value is never deleted just because it is in the way. A large unexplained residual is information about the trial, not an error.

✓Decision rule · ANOVA, transformation or ranksSection 5.5
If…then…
the grade is A or B and the response is continuousANOVA on the original scale (Block 5).
the grade is C or D and a conventional transformation raises it to A or BANOVA with the transformed response and back-transformed means.
no transformation is enoughThe non-parametric route for the design, or a generalised linear model for counts and proportions.
the response is an ordinal score (1 to 5, 1 to 9)The non-parametric route, whatever the assumptions say.
the data are normal but the variances very unequal, with a single factor and no blocksWelch with Games–Howell.

Rank tests lose little power when the data are normal (about 5% for Kruskal–Wallis compared with the F-test) and can gain a great deal when they are not.

B5Designs, ANOVA and mean comparisons

✓Decision rule · what to read first in the tableSection 6.3
If…then…
there is a significant interactionRead the cell means and the simple effects (section 6.5). Main effects on their own can be misleading.
there is no interaction and a main effect is significantSeparate the means of that factor (section 6.4).
the factor is quantitative (doses, densities, dates)Fit the trend (section 6.6) in addition to, or instead of, the letters.
the block is significantIt is not a result about the treatments: it says that blocking was worthwhile.
the treatment F is not significantDo not separate means with the LSD: the difference is not protected. Report the absence of differences with its power in mind.

In the paper, report for each factor and interaction the F with its two degrees of freedom and the P value, the CV of the trial and, preferably, the partial η².

✓Decision rule · which curve to reportSection 6.6
If the highest significant component is…then…
noneThe response does not change systematically with the dose.
linearA straight line: each unit of dose adds the same amount. There is no optimum within the range; higher doses are worth testing.
quadraticA curve with a maximum or minimum; report the equation, R² and the optimum if it falls within the range.
cubic or higherA more complex shape; with four or five doses it is usually noise or an atypical dose. Check before interpreting it.

Exact polynomial contrasts require equally spaced doses; the curve is fitted with the actual dose values.

B6Graphics of results

✓Decision rule · what a figure caption must includeSection 7.1
ElementHow to write it
What the bars are“Means ± standard error (n = 4)”. Never implicit.
What the letters mean“Means with the same letter do not differ significantly (Tukey, α = 0.05)”. The test goes in the caption, not in the title.
Double letters“ab” belongs to both groups; it is not corrected by hand.
Y axisBars start at zero; points may zoom in, and the caption says so.
UnitsIn the axis label: “Grain yield (t ha⁻¹)”.
ColourColour-blind-safe palettes (Okabe–Ito, Paul Tol) and, if the figure will be printed in grey, distinct markers or line types as well as colour.

The app's figures already state in the subtitle the type of bar and the test behind the letters. Copy that information into the caption before deleting the subtitle.

✓Decision rule · reading the residual mapSection 7.2
If the colours…then…
are scattered with no pattern, like a random chequerboardThe design absorbed the variation of the land. As expected.
form a strip of the same sign along a row or columnA gradient that blocking did not capture; in the next trial, orient the blocks perpendicular to that strip.
form a patch in one cornerA local problem (waterlogging, edge, shade); check the field notebook.
stand out in a single plotA suspect plot; compare it with the table of suspect observations in Block 4.

The Centre the colour scale at zero checkbox is ticked by default in the residual map so that the neutral colour corresponds to a zero residual.

B7Report and export

✓Decision rule · what to take from the report into a paperSection 8.3
From the report…into the manuscript…
Methods paragraphInto the materials and methods section, after adding what the app cannot know: location, soil, crop management, plot size and dates.
ANOVA tableSummarised: F with its degrees of freedom and P for each source, and the CV. The full table goes to the supplementary material.
Means with lettersInto a table in the paper, with the caption the report already provides (test, α, minimum difference).
Draft resultsAs a starting point; replace the column names with readable names and add the agronomic interpretation.
FiguresFrom the ZIP or the Block 6 gallery, at the journal’s resolution (chapter 7), not from the PDF.
Appendices and dataInto the supplementary material.

The figure captions in the report repeat the title of each figure; in the paper, rewrite them with the information in table 7.1.

B8Design generator and power

✓Decision rule · when to change the seedSection 9.2
If the layout…then…
has two neighbouring plots with the same treatmentLeave it: that is part of chance. “Improving” a randomisation by hand invalidates it.
is impossible in the field (irrigation that does not reach, machinery that does not fit)Change the seed and record in the protocol the reason and the discarded seed.
must be repeated at another locationUse a different seed at each location and record them all.

Never choose the seed by trying until the layout “looks right”: that is choosing the layout, not randomising it.

✓Decision rule · what to do with the resultSection 9.5
If the replicates required…then…
fit the land and the budgetSow at least that many, and check that the skeleton ANOVA leaves 10 to 12 degrees of freedom for error.
are twice as many as possibleWork on the CV (plots, borders, blocks) or reduce the number of treatments: fewer treatments well tested are worth more than many poorly tested.
are far more than possibleAccept a larger detectable difference and state it in the protocol, or redesign the trial across several locations or seasons.
cannot be calculated (more than 200)The difference of interest is too small for that CV; there is no point in sowing the trial that way.

An underpowered trial is no saving: if it finds no differences, there is no way to tell whether there are none or whether it could not see them.
